🧠 Pranjal Kamra's Core Investment Philosophy & Recurring Principles 6 Pillars
The immutable fundamental mental models, financial filters, and recurring rules Pranjal Kamra emphasizes across his 180+ videos:
Capital Preservation 🛡️
1. The Elimination Engine (Rejection Over Selection)
"Reject 98% of listed stocks upfront on high debt (D/E > 1.0), continuous equity dilution, or promoter pledging (>10%)."
"Stock market mein paisa banana is not about finding the next multibagger, it's about not buying the garbage. 98% companiyaan pehle din hi reject ho jaati hain."
Wealth creation starts with survival. Pranjal routinely disqualifies stocks with high Debt-to-Equity (>1.0), continuous equity dilution via QIP/Rights/FPO, high promoter pledging (>10%), or questionable corporate governance. If management must dilute shareholders to keep lights on, no valuation is cheap enough.

Structural Moat 🏰
2. The Toll-Booth Monopolies (Transaction Moats)
"Own statutory platform monopolies that collect an unskippable transaction toll whether markets go up or down."
"Mujhe aisi companiyaan pasand hain jo toll booth ki tarah hain — chahe market upar jaaye ya neeche, transaction hoga toh inka revenue banega hi."
Loves capital-light platforms protected by regulatory licenses and deep switching costs. As Indian financialization and retail demat accounts explode, toll-booth platforms collect non-linear fees on every trade, IPO, and settlement with over 80% incremental EBITDA margins and virtually zero CAPEX.

Sovereign & Price Risk 🚫
3. The Anti-PSU & Price-Capping Trap (Govt ≠ ROE)
"Avoid government utilities and price-capped PSUs whose sovereign duty is public welfare, not minority shareholder ROE."
"Government ka primary objective profit ya share price badhana nahi, public welfare hai. Jab crude badhega, sarkar OMCs ko petrol ke daam badhane nahi degi."
Systematic avoidance of PSU oil marketing firms, monopsony public suppliers, and state-regulated utilities. When commodity prices surge, governments cap retail prices to control inflation, sacrificing minority shareholder returns. Pranjal systematically favors private champions with unconstrained pricing power.

Niche Dominance 🐟
4. Big Fish in a Small Pond (Niche Monopolies)
"Own 60%–80% market share champions in niche ₹1,000–5,000 Cr sectors before institutional discovery rerates their multiples."
"Badi market mein chhota player banne se behtar hai, kisi chhoote market ka 70% share leke badshah bano."
Champions high-ROCE leaders that dominate tight, specialized niches with 60%–80% market share. Large institutional funds and foreign institutions cannot invest in them early due to small market caps (₹1,000–5,000 Cr), allowing retail investors to accumulate at reasonable multiples before inevitable institutional discovery.

Capital Efficiency ⚡
5. The Zerodha & Zoho Ethos (Negative Working Capital)
"Compound with customer-funded, negative working capital businesses (ROCE > 20%) that reject debt and cash-burning VC models."
"Jo company customers ke advance paise se grow karti hai aur negative working capital rakhti hai, use bank loan ya investor ki zarurat hi nahi padti."
Pioneered by capital-efficient titans. Prefers companies where customers pay upfront, suppliers provide favorable credit, ROCE consistently exceeds 20-25%, and Free Cash Flow exceeds 80% of reported PAT. Highly critical of cash-burning startup valuations lacking path-to-profitability.

Capital Allocation 🎯
6. Focused Purity vs. Diworsification
"Insist on pure single-category leaders; immediately exit managements that squander monopoly cash flows on unrelated vanity expansions."
"Diversification aapko portfolio level pe karni chahiye, company level pe nahi. Agar koi tyre company hotel kholne lage, toh main exit le leta hoon."
